You Can Just Download More Tokens/Sec

sentdex · sentdex · July 20, 2026 · ~52 min

Study Guide

Overview

In this frontier-AI-at-home update, sentdex makes a sustained argument that speed matters as much as intelligence when running large language models. The framing joke, that you can just download more tokens per second, captures the core idea: a fresh crop of open-weight models plus DeepSeek's new DSpark speculative-decoding release let a home rig hit inference speeds that no API provider will give you. He demonstrates a local DeepSeek V4 Flash setup running at over 300 tokens per second, then works through the hardware plumbing needed to get there, the recent model releases, and why he believes a fast, human-in-the-loop model beats a giant, slow one for most real work.

Key Concepts

Generation Speed and Time to First Token

sentdex runs a benchmark prompt live and clocks 302 then 322 tokens per second on code generation, noting that code decode tends to run almost twice as fast as regular text output. He stresses two speed axes: raw tokens per second, and time to first token, which is governed by prefill speed (how fast the model loads context into memory before generating). Prefill is normally much faster than generation.

Why Prefill Speed Suddenly Stopped Mattering

On GLM 5.2 local at four-bit, his prefill speed was about 1,100 tokens per second, so a 90,000-token context took roughly 80 seconds just to start responding. That pain was baked into his brain. With DeepSeek V4 Flash the same 90k context returned first output in about 724 milliseconds, because its prefill runs near 100,000 tokens per second and actually gets faster with larger context. The prefill speed became so good he had never bothered to check it until this video.

Speculative Decoding, MTP, and DSpark

Because these models are just next-token predictors, running every token through the full model is inefficient. Speculative decoding started as small separate draft models predicting several tokens ahead, then verified in parallel by the big model, typically yielding a 30 to 50 percent speedup. DeepSeek V4 Flash moved this into the model with MTP (multi-token prediction) layers. DSpark layers additional software optimizations and an extra speculative-decoding module on top, giving sentdex roughly another 50 percent on his hardware. He notes these techniques are theoretically lossless, so benchmark differences he saw are likely error bars.

Local Beats API on Speed and Trust

API providers, he argues, basically target the 50 to 80 tokens per second range, and even fast providers like Together fluctuate wildly (sometimes 200, sometimes 5 tokens per second depending on load). Local guarantees the precision you expect, protects your IP, and delivers speeds APIs simply will not. He also flags that some OpenRouter providers may serve FP4 while claiming FP8, distorting benchmarks, which he suspects explains a DeepSeek V4 Flash DSpark score that beat GLM 5.2.

The Hardware Story

The Rig

He runs a roughly four-and-a-half-year-old Puget build with seven PCIe 4 slots and a terabyte of ECC DDR4 RAM plus nearly 300 GB of VRAM. The demos ran on only two RTX Pro 6000s at TP2 over x16 PCIe 4. Four cards at x16 PCIe 5 could push closer to 400 tokens per second. He also notes DeepSeek cannot run at TP3 because of attention-head divisibility; MiniMax M2.7 with 48 attention heads was a rare model that allowed TP3.

Risers, SlimSAS, MCIO, and Switches

His strong opinion: risers suck. Signal integrity over distance and heat from cards baking the cables caused cards to fall off the bus and crash the machine after sustained load. The fix, sourced from cpayne.com (Christian Payne in Germany), is proper cabling. For PCIe 4 riser behavior, use SlimSAS with a passive host adapter (roughly 40 euro plus two cables). For PCIe 5 speeds you need MCIO and, for multiple cards, one or two retimers per four cards feeding a PCIe switch that fans out MCIO to gen-five adapters. A gen-five switch can even give GPUs a fast interconnect on an older gen-three motherboard, similar in spirit to NVLink.

Power and Lightning Protection

He upgraded to a double-online-conversion UPS around 4.5 to 5 kilowatts that truly isolates hardware from the grid, needed because he lives in what he calls one of the worst US locations for lightning strikes. He recommends greenlightups.com for refurbished server-grade UPS units at about half price with replacement batteries, and mentions SPDs on his PV and buried Ethernet runs.

Recent Model Releases

He surveys a fast-moving landscape: Kimi K3 (2.8 trillion parameters, open weights promised July 27, a worthy Fabled 5 / GPT 5.6 Sole competitor but too big for him to run, needing ~1.4 TB at four-bit); Qwen 3.8 (2.4 trillion parameters, potentially runnable on his rig at four-bit); Thinking Machines' Inkling from Mira Murati's team (a one-trillion model he found not worth its size, but Inkling Small at 276 billion looks strong); and Grok 4.5 with its Grok Build CLI, which allegedly uploaded entire repos and git history to xAI's cloud.

The Central Argument: Human In The Loop

sentdex contends that as models grew to 3-to-8-trillion-parameter giants like Opus 4.8 and Fabled, they became so slow that users get distracted, stop watching, and drift toward human-out-of-the-loop workflows (slash-goal and go to bed). He compares it to old people at slot machines: hit enter, wait five minutes, repeat. He argues a cheap, fast model like DeepSeek V4 Flash, run human-in-the-loop, iterates so quickly it feels as smart as Fabled 5 for his work, and that people wrongly believe they need the biggest model. His close: he does not want the AI to think for him, he wants it as an extension of himself, and offloading thinking to ever-bigger models is the wrong trade.

Privacy

Beyond the Grok Build incident, he reframes the concern: any API use already sends your entire files, IP, and the way you think as context. Grok Build was just the most in-your-face version of what has been happening all along, which is another reason to run local.

Practical Takeaways

  • Judge models on both tokens per second and time to first token, not intelligence alone.
  • Prefill speed dominates the wait on large contexts; a fast-prefill model (DeepSeek V4 Flash near 100k tok/s) makes big context nearly free versus GLM 5.2 at ~1,100 tok/s.
  • DSpark on DeepSeek V4 Flash gave about 50 percent more speed on top of MTP; speculative decoding is theoretically lossless.
  • Two RTX Pro 6000s at TP2 hit 300-plus tok/s single-stream and still served ~8 concurrent users at ~150 tok/s each.
  • Avoid risers; use SlimSAS for PCIe 4 and MCIO plus retimers and a PCIe switch for PCIe 5. cpayne.com is his sourcing recommendation.
  • Use a proper double-conversion UPS; consider refurbished units from greenlightups.com.
  • API providers target only 50 to 80 tok/s and can be inconsistent; local guarantees precision, speed, and privacy.
  • Try DeepSeek V4 Flash over an API first (hard to spend $200 a month) before assuming you need Fabled or GPT 5.6 Sole.
  • Any API call already ships your files and IP as context, so weigh local for privacy reasons.
