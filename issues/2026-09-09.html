AI Research · Controversy

OpenAI Fought Dirty on Career-Making Math Problem, Says NYU Mathematician

TechCrunch · Sep 8

NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster alleges that OpenAI obtained information about his and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge's approach to the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem, then used superior computing resources to publish a full proof first. Buckmaster says the episode came with pressure and intimidation from OpenAI leadership, turning what should have been a landmark mathematical result into a dispute over how the work was obtained.

Buckmaster laid out his account in detail in a statement posted to his NYU page, describing the LLM-assisted proof work and the dispute in his own words. OpenAI has published its own post about the Navier-Stokes solution, presenting it as the company's achievement; that page did not load for verification, so its framing is noted here only as OpenAI's own claim, not as corroboration of either account. (See Buckmaster's full statement (PDF) for the source account.)

AI Security

Hackers Are Stealing Claude Tokens From Subscribers

TechCrunch · Sep 8

Hackers are exploiting compromised Claude session keys and infostealer malware to quietly burn through subscribers' token allowances. Anthropic has confirmed the attacks and acknowledged that its current detection tools aren't adequate to catch the abuse before subscribers notice their usage has been drained.

AI for Science

AlphaGenome Atlas Maps the Effects of Nearly Every Possible Human DNA Variant

Google (The Keyword) · Sep 8

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a database predicting the effects of roughly 9 billion possible single nucleotide variants across the human genome. Each variant gets an AlphaGenome Variant Impact (AVI) score, giving researchers a way to prioritize which genetic variants are worth chasing in disease and trait research.

Developer Tools

deltafin: Running a 2.8T-Parameter Model on a MacBook

GitHub · Sep 8

A Rust project called deltafin runs the full Kimi K3 language model, all 2.8 trillion parameters, on consumer Apple Silicon by streaming expert weights from SSD. It manages roughly 1 token per second of decode on an M5 Max MacBook Pro with 128GB of RAM, without pruning the model down.
