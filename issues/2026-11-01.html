✦ The Big Picture

Almost $200 billion in data center projects were delayed, stalled, or blocked in the first half of 2026 because of political and community opposition, according to a Data Center Watch report cited by TechCrunch. Yesterday’s issue was about whether the companies building them can afford the bill. Today’s lead is about whether the neighbors will let them finish.

Today’s Headlines

The Buildout Meets the Ballot Box

  • Data centers on the ballot – TechCrunch’s Dominic-Madori Davis moderated a data center panel at a Brooklyn midterm event five days before the November 3 election. When she asked who in the room was pro-AI data center, she was booed. Panelist Hilary Flint, an environmental and public health advocate, said people protest because they “value their lives a little bit more than a temporary check.” The panel’s shared view was that data centers have become the physical embodiment of anger at the tech class. Davis also notes that reports show data center jobs are plentiful during construction but not permanent afterward.
  • Labor and consent – NewsNation’s Batya Ungar-Sargon argued that some unions back data centers because past generations took pride in huge buildings that meant prosperity. Vox’s Astead Herndon countered that the issue is consent, noting Vivek Ramaswamy was booed in Ohio for a similar argument and that lack of consent runs across party lines. States including Pennsylvania, Georgia, Texas, New York, and Virginia are weighing more guardrails, and hundreds of new data centers are planned in Virginia alone.
  • South Korea’s export surge – Reuters’ headline says South Korea’s October exports nearly doubled on the AI investment boom. We could see only the headline, but it is the supply-side mirror of the lead: the spending that frightens voters is showing up as revenue for chip exporters.

Agents and Competition

  • DeepSeek’s coding agent – Bloomberg reports that DeepSeek has released an enhanced coding agent aimed at rivaling Anthropic. The article was behind a paywall, so we cannot say what the agent does or how it benchmarks. The headline alone tells you that agentic coding, not chat, is now where Chinese labs are choosing to compete.
  • Five tasks for agents – The New York Times offers a list of five tasks it suggests giving to AI agents. We saw only the headline, but a mainstream paper running agent how-tos shows the topic has moved from developer circles to general readers.

Security

  • Binance hack attribution – The AP headline says the FBI is blaming North Korea for billions stolen from Binance in what it calls the biggest hack in history. We could not open the article, and a quick search did not independently confirm it, so read the AP report before repeating the figure.

The Wire

  • Politics and courts – Reuters and AP carry stories on a $50 million contract for ex-lawyer Habba Mildon that Trump called “not acceptable,” California’s governor asking federal judges to resist attempts to seize ballots, Flavio Bolsonaro staying in the U.S., a judge dismissing Trump’s suit against the Wall Street Journal over the Epstein letter, an FBI visit to a donor in the Alaska Senate inquiry involving Dan Sullivan, and a Texas and Michigan midterm roundup.
  • Trade and crime – Bloomberg reports that China’s upgraded Peru trade deal has taken effect, widening ties. AP reports federal officials are investigating arson at a Mississippi police department.
  • Sports – The Valkyries won the WNBA title over the Dream 68-63 in Game 7, No. 4 Indiana beat Minnesota 23-16 in overtime, USC upset No. 2 Ohio State 30-18, LeBron James had his best game yet with the 76ers, and the Jets placed T’Vondre Sweat on injured reserve.

The Throughline

Put today’s lead next to yesterday’s news and the AI buildout has two bills coming due at once. Yesterday the problem was financial: negative free cash flow at the biggest spenders and record debt raises. Today it is political. The $200 billion in blocked or delayed projects is a number about permission, not money, and it arrives just as voters in New York are booing a pro-data-center question in a room that is full of techies.

The sharpest point in the TechCrunch piece is Herndon’s, because it moves the argument off jobs and onto consent. Ungar-Sargon’s Rust Belt pride argument assumes communities were asked and said yes. Davis’s panel described the opposite: secret deals with local officials and zoning that hides what is being built until it is too late to protest. If that is the grievance, more construction jobs will not fix it, and the industry’s usual answer is aimed at the wrong complaint.

The two stories we could read only by headline fit the same picture from the other side. South Korea’s export jump and DeepSeek’s coding agent both say the demand and competition are real. What the lead adds is that the physical footprint of that demand has a local address, and the people at that address are now voting.

The Bigger Picture

Technology buildouts have usually run into local opposition eventually, from railroads to power lines. What is different here is speed and visibility: a data center is a large, windowless building that arrives with little public input, and it has become a stand-in for a wider anxiety about who gains from AI. Davis’s observation that people outside the tech world find it all “ugly” is the part the industry should take most seriously.

If the midterms show data centers can swing races, expect state legislatures to treat transparency and siting rules as popular politics rather than technical policy. That would raise the cost and slow the timeline of the buildout in exactly the states, like Virginia, where capacity is concentrated. Whether that is a pause or a real brake depends on the results on November 3, which we cannot yet read.

What to Watch

  • November 3 results. Watch whether candidates who ran against local data centers outperform, and how the industry and state officials describe the outcome afterward.
  • State guardrails. Pennsylvania, Georgia, Texas, New York, and Virginia are all weighing transparency or oversight rules. Watch which bills move first once the election is over.
  • DeepSeek’s agent in practice. Wait for independent testing and developer reports before treating the release as a threat to Anthropic or anyone else. We have only a paywalled headline.
