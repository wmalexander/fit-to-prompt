✦ The Big Picture

The only figure we could verify today comes from TechCrunch: a U.S. startup is seeking $162 million in damages from Apple in India, plus an injunction that would affect iPhone and Apple Watch imports, over a tap-to-share contacts feature. Everything else in this issue is a headline we could not read past, and the headlines share a mood: the AI trade is being argued over, whether in a trading debut, a chip shipment or a proof.

Today’s Headlines

A note on sources: The Wall Street Journal, The Washington Post, The New York Times and Bloomberg all blocked automated access when this issue was built, and the Google News links for Journal and Post stories do not expose article text. Only the TechCrunch story was read. Everything else below comes from headlines, so treat it as a map of what each piece covers and read the originals for the substance.

Anthropic and the Market

  • A rough debut. The Journal’s headline says the blame game has begun over Anthropic’s turbulent trading debut. A second Journal headline profiles a brother and sister who want to keep Anthropic “weird.”
  • Wider jitters. The Post’s headline says U.S. stocks slumped into correction territory amid tech and AI worries.

Nvidia, China and Oversight

  • Chip sales. The Post reports that officials approved the sale of 580,000 Nvidia chips to Chinese companies, per its headline.
  • The spying allegation. The Times says Alice Huang, accused of spying on Nvidia’s CEO, is linked to firms with ties to China’s military.
  • Defense start-ups. The Post says a Pentagon watchdog will audit deals with defense tech start-ups.

Math, Networks and Legal Fights

  • A Riemann claim. Bloomberg says an Anthropic researcher claims to have solved a Riemann problem with Claude, and the Post describes a mathematician who used AI and claims to have solved one of math’s great mysteries. Whether these are the same claim, and whether it holds up, is not something the headlines settle.
  • Apple in India. The $162 million suit described above is the one story here we read in full.
  • Also on the wire. China deploying 5G across its manufacturing base as 6G testing advances, per CCTV via Bloomberg, and a Bloomberg Wall Street Week episode on cybersecurity meeting AI.

The Throughline

Three of today’s headlines are about scrutiny: of a trading debut, of chip sales to China, and of Pentagon contracts with start-ups. When a technology becomes both an asset class and a strategic material, it draws auditors, investigators and critics at the same time. We cannot say more than the headlines allow.

The Riemann headlines are the odd ones out, and the most worth reading carefully. A claim to have solved a famous problem with a model is easy to announce and slow to verify, so the honest posture is to wait for mathematicians to check it.

The Bigger Picture

Over the past few issues the story has moved from a record offering to its first trading days. A headline about a blame game suggests the easy phase is over, and that expectations, not just products, now move AI companies.

Meanwhile the chip headlines show that the hardware side stays entangled with politics. That is a pattern, not a prediction, and a thin day of sourcing is a reason to hold conclusions loosely.

What to Watch

  • Verification of the Riemann claim. Look for independent mathematicians weighing in.
  • Anthropic’s next trading sessions. The Journal and Post headlines point to a nervous market.
  • Nvidia and China. The sale approvals and the spying allegation may draw follow-up from officials.
