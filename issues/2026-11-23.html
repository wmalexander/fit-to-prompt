✦ The Big Picture

Futures were up nearly 3% on the Nasdaq Sunday night, but the number worth holding onto is Moody’s Mark Zandi’s: if stocks stay where they are, the wealth effect shaves almost 0.3 percentage points off real GDP growth a year from now. Meanwhile the three investors who bet billions on Anthropic are sitting on paper gains of roughly 15 times for the earliest of them. Same week, same industry, two very different feelings about what AI is worth.

Today’s Headlines

A note on sources: Bloomberg blocked automated access when this issue was built, so its two items are summarized from their published headlines and feed descriptions. A fifth queued story, from The Washington Post, was left out because its headline could not be confirmed. The Fortune and TechCrunch items are drawn from the articles themselves.

The Selloff and the Bounce

  • Stocks rebound from correction. Fortune reports Dow futures up 798 points (1.66%), S&P 500 futures up 2.11% and Nasdaq futures up 2.75%. That follows the Dow’s and Nasdaq’s biggest weekly declines since the early days of the pandemic and the S&P 500’s steepest since April 2025’s tariff shock. The article ties the slide to fears that the AI boom is about to bust, which began after midterm wins by Democrats seen as more skeptical of data centers, then deepened with the Nvidia spy scandal that ousted Jensen Huang and worries over “circular” financing deals. Thanksgiving trading is thin: closed Thursday, a half day Friday.
  • Forecasters stay bullish. Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs see the S&P 500 at 8,700 at the end of 2027, UBS Global Wealth Management and Wells Fargo at 8,200, and Federated Hermes’ Steve Chiavarone at 10,000, a 45% jump from current levels. He argues the data-center buildout risks are overblown and that a modest pullback “could give cash flows time to catch up.”
  • Emerging markets join in. Bloomberg says emerging-market stocks are set to erase this year’s gains as doubts about the sustainability of the AI trade fuel a global selloff stretching from the US to Asia.

Anthropic and the Money Behind It

  • Who bet early. TechCrunch says Notable Capital put roughly 10% of the U.S. portion of its $1 billion-plus fund into Anthropic’s Series E at a $61.5 billion valuation, its highest entry price in 25 years, after its team found developers using Claude 70% of the time. Anthropic then raised $81.3 billion in the largest IPO in history at a fully diluted valuation of about $1.5 trillion, a paper return of roughly 15 times.
  • The patient money. Singapore’s GIC first invested in Series F at $183 billion, led Series G with Coatue at $380 billion and co-led the pre-IPO round at $965 billion, more than $4.5 billion in all for a stake of about 1%. Baillie Gifford put in $1.5 billion over three rounds. Both cite consistent execution, enterprise adoption and Claude Code’s early traction with developers.

Defense

  • Agon raises $70 million. Bloomberg reports the UK startup, which makes simulation software for the European defense industry, raised a Series A from investors including Roelof Botha, Sequoia Capital’s former head.

The Throughline

Read Fortune and TechCrunch side by side and you get the same asset priced two ways. The early Anthropic backers were rewarded for conviction that developers would pull AI into real work, and the IPO turned that into a number the whole market can see. Fortune’s market report describes the opposite fear: that the buildout financed around that conviction runs ahead of the cash flows. Both can be true, and the week suggests they are.

Zandi’s arithmetic is the sober part. A 2-cents-per-dollar wealth effect means a stock market sitting on AI gains is part of the economy’s demand, not just a scoreboard. His line that “the easy money in the AI trade has been made” is a claim about returns, not about technology. The Agon raise shows capital still flowing to specific, concrete uses in defense even as the broad trade wobbles.

The Bigger Picture

Two years ago the question was whether AI companies could raise money at all. Now the largest IPO on record has happened and the market fell hard within days. That is a normal pattern for a technology moving from private promise to public accountability: prices start answering to quarterly evidence rather than to narratives.

The politics matter as much as the earnings. Fortune places the start of this slide after an election that elevated data-center skeptics, plus export-control and scandal headlines. Whether the buildout slows modestly, as Chiavarone suggests, or sharply is now a policy question as well as a financial one.

What to Watch

  • Whether the bounce holds. A thin holiday week can exaggerate rebounds in both directions, so look for follow-through after Friday’s half day.
  • Data-center funding. Watch for any pullback in buildout plans or financing, since Fortune says that is the market’s central worry.
  • Anthropic’s first public stretch. How the stock trades against its roughly $1.5 trillion valuation will shape how the early backers’ gains are read.
